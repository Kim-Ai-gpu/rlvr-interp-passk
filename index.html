Abstract

As test-time scaling (TTS) emerges as a new scaling axis, a model's sample diversity (pass@k at large k) has become as important as its single-sample accuracy (pass@1) in the many applications that combine a verifier with multiple reasoning traces. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), however, improves pass@1 while narrowing this diversity (Yue et al., 2025), which makes test-time scaling less sample-efficient. We apply WiSE-FT-style weight interpolation, W(α) = Wbase + α(WRL − Wbase), as a single training-free knob that trades pass@1 for pass@k. Under the experimental setup of Yue et al. (Qwen2.5-7B and SimpleRL-Zoo-7B on six math benchmarks), α = 0.25 recovers the base model's large-k coverage (pass@128 of 70.4 vs. 70.3 for the base model and 63.8 for RL, averaged over the two non-saturated benchmarks) while keeping 64–80% of the RL pass@1 gain, and it reaches the RL model's pass@128 with 2.3–7.1× fewer samples. α = 0.5 strikes a balance (pass@1 −1.2 and large-k pass@k +1.6 points relative to RL), and α = 0.75 is nearly a free lunch (RL-level pass@1 with OlympiadBench pass@128 +2.5). The coverage reached at α = 0.25 exceeds what the RL model reaches at any sampling temperature reported for this pair.

Target

Read the ratios with care: the RLVR policy's pass@k curve is flat at large k, so small differences in pass@k become large differences in the required k. The base model also needs few samples, but on OlympiadBench, Minerva, and MATH500 its pass@1 is 8 to 13 points lower than that of α = 0.25. AMC23 and AIME24 are omitted because of their size (40 and 30 problems).

Why mixing is not the same as heating

To first order, the interpolated model's next-token distribution is a weighted product of the two models, πα ∝ π01−α π1α. Temperature rescales the RLVR policy alone, π11/T. Move the sliders on the worked example from the paper.

Which α should I use?

Recommendations from the 7B pair. On a new pair, treat them as a starting point to confirm.

0.25

Verifier, many samples

0.5

Balance

What it does not do

Stated up front in the paper.

  • No new abilities. No interpolated model beats both endpoints; most of the effect matches a mixture of samples from the two models, but from a single model.
  • Needs a verifier. Under majority voting, interpolation does not improve on the RLVR policy.
  • Narrow scope. One pair of 7B models, math only, one sampling seed.
  • Needs the base model. The weights RLVR started from must be public.
  • Use fp16 or fp32. The RLVR update is small; bf16 rounding erases most of it (in a check on a 1.5B pair, only 29% survived at α = 0.25).
